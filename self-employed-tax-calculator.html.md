## What 2026/27 rates does it use?

| Item | 2026/27 |
|---|---|
| Personal Allowance | £12,570 (reduced by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income over £100,000) |
| Basic / higher / additional rate | 20% to £50,270 · 40% to £125,140 · 45% above |
| Class 4 NI | 6% on £12,570–£50,270 · 2% above |
| Class 2 NI | Treated as paid if profits £7,105+; voluntary £3.65/week below |

Simplification: Class 4 is worked out on profit alone. When you have a salary too, the extra Income Tax is shown as the tax on (salary + profit) minus the tax on salary alone. Payments on account are not included.

## What is the formula, in plain text?

Profit = turnover − allowable expenses. Income Tax = 20% of profit from £12,570 to £50,270 + 40% to £125,140 + 45% above (Personal Allowance tapers by £1 per £2 over £100,000). Class 4 NI = 6% of profit from £12,570 to £50,270 + 2% above. With other income, the extra Income Tax = tax on (other income + profit) − tax on other income alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much tax does a self-employed person pay in 2026/27?

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland you pay Income Tax on profits above the £12,570 Personal Allowance at 20% up to £50,270, 40% up to £125,140 and 45% above. You also pay Class 4 National Insurance of 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above £50,270.

### Do I still pay Class 2 National Insurance?

No, not if your profits are £7,105 or more. GOV.UK says Class 2 is treated as paid to protect your National Insurance record. Below £7,105 you can choose to pay voluntary Class 2 at £3.65 a week in 2026/27.

### Is tax worked out on turnover or profit?

On profit: your self-employed income minus allowable expenses. You can use the £1,000 trading allowance instead of claiming expenses.

### Does this cover Scotland?

No. Scotland has different Income Tax bands. Class 4 National Insurance is the same across the UK.
