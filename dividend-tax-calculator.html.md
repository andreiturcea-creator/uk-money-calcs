## How is it calculated?

Your Personal Allowance is used against salary first, then dividends. The first £500 of dividends is taxed at 0% but still uses up band. Dividends that fall in the basic band are taxed at 10.75%, the higher band at 35.75% and the additional band at 39.35%. This does not work out the Income Tax on your salary itself. Scotland: dividend rates are UK-wide, but this tool uses the rUK bands to decide which band applies.

Example from GOV.UK: £29,570 wages and £3,000 dividends gives £0 on £500 and 10.75% on £2,500 of dividends.

## What is the formula, in plain text?

Personal Allowance is used against salary first, then dividends. The first £500 of dividends is taxed at 0% but still uses band. Remaining dividends are taxed at 10.75% within the basic band (first £37,700 of taxable income), 35.75% up to £125,140 total income and 39.35% above.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the dividend allowance for 2026/27?

£500. You pay no tax on the first £500 of dividends, though they still count towards your tax band.

### What are the dividend tax rates for 2026/27?

GOV.UK lists 10.75% for the basic rate, 35.75% for the higher rate and 39.35% for the additional rate, from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027.

### Do I pay tax on dividends in an ISA?

No. Dividends from shares held in an ISA are tax-free.

### Do I pay National Insurance on dividends?

No. National Insurance is not charged on dividends, which is why many company directors take a small salary and the rest as dividends.
